# Fast Pleasures and Awareness

The question starts from a premise. If anything is the point of a life, the candidate is **awareness** — being present to what is actually happening, moment to moment. Everything else (building, loving, serving, producing) is not separate from this; those activities are modes of awareness. When you build something, there is an awareness through which the building happens. When you love someone, there is an awareness in which the love is felt. The substrate is the same. Awareness is the thing living; the rest is what it lives through. [[compass-not-map]] reaches this conclusion from a different angle — life as the experience of consciousness, not the collection of residue. The word being sharpened here is *awareness*, because it has cleaner edges and because it names what actually varies.

If that premise holds, a question follows about activities like scrolling short-form video, sugar, casual drug use, smoking, and subtler cognitive forms like music used as a soundtrack for imagined lives. Do they add to awareness, take from it, or something else?

## The premise doesn't condemn them on its own

A first attempt at the answer runs into trouble. If awareness is the substrate, and these activities are experienced, then they too are modes of awareness. You are aware while scrolling. You are aware while high. The premise alone cannot pick out some experiences as better than others; something else has to do that work.

The move that does the work is to notice that *awareness is not uniform*. Some activities leave it intact, some expand it, some dim it, some overpower it entirely. The useful cut is not between experience and non-experience. It is between activities that awareness survives and activities that drown it.

## A pattern, not a list

The activities that drown awareness share a pattern. Four features, arriving together:

- **Awareness-reducing.** Part of the appeal is precisely that you are no longer quite present. Presence is not replaced by something richer — it is simply turned down.
- **Spiky.** Quick up, quick down. High peak, deep trough. Nothing sustained; nothing arrives.
- **Non-gratifying.** Each hit does not satisfy. It raises the floor for the next, so the next is required to reach the same signal. Appetite is increased rather than answered.
- **Compulsive.** The loop overpowers the chooser. You did not decide on the fifth video; the fifth video was the shape the organism was going to take once the first had played.

Call this **the pattern**. It can run on biochemistry — cigarettes, sugar, scrolling, the tenth drink. It can also run on cognition alone — music used not to listen to but to soundtrack an imagined life played out in the head, an hour gone into a scenario no body ever had. The mind can run a spike-and-crash pattern on itself without any molecule. The pattern is the subject; the substrate is incidental.

## The activities are not the subject

*The same activity can land in or out of the pattern depending on how you meet it.* Doom-scrolling is the pattern. Opening Instagram to research a specific question is not. A drink at a dinner full of conversation is not the pattern. A drink taken alone to dull an evening is.

So "fast pleasure" is a **relation**, not a fixed list of activities. The list is just the set of activities that commonly land in the pattern. What makes something a fast pleasure is the pattern, not the activity.

## A limit to the principle

*Intention holds the line* is a strong principle, but it has an edge. For most of these activities, the activity is a *trigger* for the pattern — the biochemistry fires, but there is a parallel channel (a conversation, a meal, music that is actually being listened to, a person present) that awareness can occupy while the chemistry runs its course. Intention loads that parallel channel. The pattern fires; awareness is simply elsewhere, and the activity is held inside a frame.

Some activities don't have a parallel channel. The activity is *itself* the loop — the biochemistry of the activity and the mechanism of the pattern are the same motion, and there is nothing co-happening for awareness to occupy. In that class, intention can decide whether to begin, but the shape once begun is forced. The frame has nothing to hold. *Intention holds the line* covers most fast pleasures well and a smaller class not at all — and it is worth naming that boundary instead of pretending the principle is universal.

The diagnostic falls out cleanly: *were you in awareness when this began, and are you in awareness when it ends?* If yes to both, whatever happened was not the pattern. If awareness was dimmed going in, or gone by the end, the pattern was running, whatever the activity was called. This is a species of the examination [[the-scripts-we-inherit]] recommends for any unendorsed program — the script here is biochemical or cognitive rather than cultural, but the test is the same.

## Purposes are real

Fast pleasures serve purposes. Pretending otherwise weakens the critique.

- **Release valve.** Effort has a ceiling. After it, the organism wants nothing to do with effort for a while. Something that asks nothing of you is what is being asked for.
- **Low-grade rest.** Not true rest — the body knows the difference — but a plausible approximation, and more available than actual rest.
- **Social lubricant.** Shared cigarettes, shared drinks, shared scroll-laughs. The activity is a cover for being in each other's company.
- **Gateway.** Idle music has sent some people to making music. Scrolling has surfaced a question someone ended up studying for years. Sugar in childhood has produced bakers in adulthood. A fast pleasure is not always terminal.

These are real. A critique that ignores them misses what the activities are actually doing in a life.

The honest critique is narrower and truer: **fast pleasures are often used for purposes they cannot fulfill**. Scrolling is used for rest; the scroll does not restore the nervous system the way rest does. Sugar is used for comfort; the comfort sugar gives is the chemical signature of being comforted, not the condition of being comforted. Likes and validation-scrolling are used for connection; a thumbs-up is not being known by anyone. The substitution is not in the molecule; it is in what the activity is being asked to do. The activity is asked to deliver a thing it physically cannot, and delivers instead the *feeling* of having received it.

This is the counterfeit. Not the activity itself — the mismatch between what it is asked for and what it can give. The hit suppresses the demand enough to keep the real thing from being sought. The craving that might otherwise have led to intimacy, rest, or genuine comfort is spent on something that imitates them without producing them. This is the same substitution structure [[reflections-on-ivan-ilyich]] describes at the scale of a whole life — Ivan's performed kindness, performed career, performed marriage, all experienced only as their performance. Fast pleasures are that same move compressed into an afternoon.

## Intention holds the line

What separates a fast pleasure from an instrumental use of the same activity is intention. Research on Instagram has a specific question behind it; doom-scrolling has no behind. A ritualised chemical experience has a frame; a compulsive one does not. A cigarette inside a conversation is part of the conversation; a cigarette while pacing alone is a loop firing.

Intention is not the same as willpower. It is closer to a question the activity is being asked: *what am I doing with this right now?* An activity under intention is being used. An activity without intention is using you. The line is whether the organism is holding the activity, or the activity is holding the organism.

This is what makes "are fast pleasures bad?" a malformed question. The activity is not the unit of judgment. The relation is.

A level beneath this distinction sits a broader one. Intention is itself a shape of wanting — the Buddhist vocabulary calls it *chanda*, desire that holds awareness in the doing. Its opposite is *tanha*, craving that is by its mechanism elsewhere. [[craving-and-the-unlived-present]] takes up that cut: the critique of fast pleasures here becomes, there, a critique of wanting itself when wanting builds an imagined state the mind inhabits instead of the present one.

## Flow, inverted

The opposite of the fast-pleasure pattern is not abstinence. It is the state in which awareness is at maximum — the absorbed, unselfconscious engagement [[the-self-monitoring-trap]] describes as flow. Fast pleasures look like flow from the outside: time loss, absorption, the self quieted. But the mechanism is inverted. Flow absorbs the self because the activity demands the full capacity of the organism. Fast pleasures absorb the self because nothing is being demanded of it. Flow is awareness at maximum operating width; the fast-pleasure pattern is awareness turned down to its lowest useful setting. The two can feel identical at a given moment and leave opposite trails: aliveness after flow, hollowness after the loop. The subjective ledger tracks something real.

## Back to awareness

If awareness is the point, fast pleasures sit in a specific place. They are not evil, not a moral failure, not a form of sin. They are the pattern most reliably engineered — by chemistry or by the mind's own habits — to dim awareness while leaving the subjective sense that something is happening. They can be a tool. They become a trap when they displace the awareness they claim to deliver, and substitute the feeling of having been present for the fact of it.

The question originally asked — do they add to life or take from it? — has a sharper form now. They do not take the hours. The hours remain on the clock. They often take the *awareness* that would otherwise have filled the hours, and the harm, when it occurs, is not lost time. It is the dimming of the very thing the life was supposed to be for.
